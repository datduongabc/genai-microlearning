# One subject on AWS, 2026-11-19

The stack was built from an empty account (main `871c084`, ap-southeast-1, Jev reranker, chat and modules on `openai-fast`, GPT-6 Luna).
Everything was driven from a laptop in Vietnam through the public CloudFront address, as a lecturer would use it.
One subject, operating systems (5 PDFs, 15.7 MB), because it has the fixed chat questions with their source chunks, so the numbers compare with the local ones.
Raw rows: `2026-11-19-aws/`.

### Ragas, kept as raw data only

`chat_eval_ragas.py --judge gemini-flash` on the 10 answers (`ragas-gemini-flash.jsonl`): faithfulness 0.785 (0.570-1.000), answer relevancy 0.869 (0.820-0.904), no refusal.

This is not a quality figure, and it does not compare with GPT-6 Luna's 0.917 and 0.776 in `2026-11-16-four-chat-models.md`, which is the quality measure of the current default:

- Through the public API the judge sees only the passages the answer cited (one to three), not the eight the model was given, so a claim backed by an uncited passage counts as unsupported.
- The judge differs (Gemini 3.8 Flash here, Claude Haiku 5.5 there), and there are 10 questions of one subject against 50 over five.
- One answer worked out the Round Robin exercise (average waiting time 21.4 ms), and Ragas scores each step of the working as a claim the slides do not state (0.38).

What this session checks is that the deployment answers as on the laptop: every answer cited the source chunk and none refused.

## Load

40 lecturers were made through the administrator's API (`drive_generation.py seed-http`), each with a course and the first three pages of the Chapter 3 lecture.

### Reads

`api_read.js` (three list and status reads per iteration, no think time), `api-read-k6.txt`:

| Virtual users | Length | api tasks | Requests/s | p50 | p95 | Failed |
| ---: | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 50 | 60 s | 1 | 124 | 1.11 s | 1.55 s | 1 of 7,548 |
| 100 | 60 s | 1 | 123 | 2.25 s | 2.91 s | 1 of 7,665 |
| 100 | 7 min | 1, then 2 | 163 | 1.86 s | 2.74 s | 2 of 68,841 |

One api task (1 vCPU) saturates at about 124 requests a second: doubling the users doubled the wait, not the throughput.
Under the 7 minute run the CPU target (60 %) asked for a second task after about 3 minutes, and it served from about 4.5 minutes (`api-scaling.txt`).
The local figure, 220 requests a second, came from one container on the laptop's faster cores with no network between.

### Chat

`drive_generation.py chat-stream --concurrency 20 --messages 40` (`chat.json`), real model: 40 of 40 answered, first word p50 2.2 s and p95 3.6 s, whole answer p95 4.1 s, 279 messages a minute.
A first try lost 9 messages to expired 15 minute access tokens of the first accounts made; the driver now signs them in again (`login-http`).

## Restore (RTO, RPO)

A point-in-time restore of the live RDS instance to a second instance (`restore-db-instance-to-point-in-time --use-latest-restorable-time`, same subnet group, security group and parameter group), started at about 02:07:30 UTC:

- Available after 669 s (11 min).
- Restored to 02:00:36, so about 7 minutes of writes would have been lost at that moment.
- Row counts, read from inside the VPC by a one-off task: the copy held the database as it was at 02:00:36 (1 user, 5 documents, 147 chunks, 13 chat messages), against the live one at the time of reading (41 users, 45 documents, 227 chunks, 20 chat messages).

The drill instance was deleted afterwards.

### Whole drill, restore and switch

The drill was run again end to end the same morning, with the services moved onto the copy and back (scratchpad scripts of the session: the restore step above, then a switch that rewrites the three database URLs in SSM and redeploys `api` and `worker`).

- Restore started at about 02:58:30 UTC, available after 790 s (13 min), restored to 02:50:38, so about 8 minutes behind.
- Switch: from the first SSM write to `api` and `worker` stable on new tasks and `/api/ready` answering 200 through CloudFront, 296 s.
  A one-off task then read the three URLs: all three named `microlearning-demo-restore-drill`.
  Signing in as the measurement administrator, listing its courses and the 121 accounts worked on the copy.
- Switching back took 296 s too; a one-off task read the live host in all three URLs, and the drill instance was deleted.

So the measured recovery time is 669 to 790 s of restore plus 296 s of switch, 16 to 18 minutes, and the recovery point was 7 to 8 minutes behind.
The copy's data could not be told from the live one in the app, because the restore point came after the session's last writes; the row counts of the first drill show a copy as of its restore point.
